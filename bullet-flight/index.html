Bullet Flight: Yaw, Precession & Nutation

Explore how a spinning projectile can remain pointed close to its direction of travel while its axis undergoes yaw, precession and nutation.

Motion
Spin Rotation about the bullet's own longitudinal axis.
Yaw The angle between the bullet's longitudinal axis and its direction of travel.
Precession The bullet axis slowly rotates around the direction-of-travel axis, tracing a cone.
Nutation A smaller oscillation superimposed on the precessional motion.
The dashed centreline is the velocity direction. The projectile's longitudinal axis precesses around it.
Watch the projectile axis

The bullet spins rapidly about its own axis, but that axis does not have to point exactly along the direction of travel.

A yawed bullet can undergo slower precession, causing its longitudinal axis to sweep around the flight direction. Nutation produces a smaller oscillation in the yaw angle during this motion.

Spin is rotation about the projectile itself. Precession and nutation describe motion of the projectile's axis.
This is a conceptual teaching model. The motion is exaggerated and slowed so that spin, yaw, precession and nutation can be distinguished visually.
