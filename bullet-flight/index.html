Spin Rapid rotation about the projectile's own longitudinal axis.

Precession A slower rotation of the projectile axis around the direction of travel.

Tumbling Large angular motion after stable nose-forward flight is lost.
The end-on trace combines a slower precessional rotation with a faster, smaller nutational motion. With nutation switched off, the trace becomes a simple circle.
Two angular motions are superimposed

Precession produces the larger, slower rotation of the projectile axis around the direction of travel.

Nutation is a smaller, faster angular motion superimposed on that precession. Together they can produce the characteristic looping or rosette-like path of the projectile axis.

Switching nutation off leaves only the slow precessional mode, so the end-on trace becomes a simple circular orbit.

Nutation is not simply a change in the radius of precession. It is a second angular mode superimposed on the precessional motion.
This is a conceptual teaching model. The relative frequencies and amplitudes are deliberately chosen to make the two angular modes easy to distinguish and are not intended to represent a specific projectile.
