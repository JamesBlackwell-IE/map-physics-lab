Spin Rapid rotation about the projectile's own longitudinal axis.
Yaw The angular separation between the projectile axis and its direction of travel.
Precession A slower rotation of the projectile axis around the direction of travel.
Nutation A faster, smaller angular oscillation superimposed on the precession.
Tumbling Large angular motion after stable nose-forward flight is lost.
The end-on trace combines a slower precessional mode with a faster, smaller nutational mode. In unstable flight, the angular motion grows and gradually transitions into tumbling.
From stable angular motion to tumbling

Precession produces the larger, slower rotation of the projectile axis around the direction of travel.

Nutation is a smaller, faster angular mode superimposed on the precession. Together they produce the looping or rosette-like path in the end-on view.

During unstable flight, the angular excursion grows. The projectile progressively loses its nose-forward orientation before entering continuous end-over-end tumbling.

Yaw, precession and nutation can all occur during stable flight. Instability occurs when the angular motion grows rather than remaining bounded.
This is a conceptual teaching model. The relative frequencies, amplitudes and transition times are deliberately chosen to make the different angular motions easy to distinguish and do not represent a specific projectile.
