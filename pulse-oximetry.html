Why two colours, and why a pulse?

Two colours: deoxygenated haemoglobin absorbs more red light at 660 nm than oxygenated haemoglobin. At 940 nm, oxygenated haemoglobin absorbs more than deoxygenated haemoglobin. Their different responses allow an estimate of oxygen saturation.

The pulse: arterial blood volume changes with each heartbeat. More arterial blood absorbs more light, so less reaches the detector. Tissue and non-pulsating blood contribute to the steady background.

The device compares the size of the pulsating change with the steady signal at each wavelength, then compares these two fractions. Real devices convert this ratio into SpO₂ using calibration data.

What the number means: SpO₂ estimates arterial haemoglobin oxygen saturation. It does not directly measure oxygen concentration in tissue, total blood oxygen content or carbon dioxide.
